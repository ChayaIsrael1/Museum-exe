Vincent Willem Van Gogh

incent van Gogh (1853–1890) was a Dutch Post-Impressionist painter whose work had a monumental influence on 20th-century art. Known for his raw emotional intensity, bold colors, and dramatic, swirling brushwork, he created iconic masterpieces like The Starry Night and Sunflowers. Although he struggled with severe mental illness and sold only one painting during his lifetime, he is now recognized globally as a tragic genius and a pioneer of Expressionism.

Claude Monet

Claude Monet (1840–1926) was a French painter and a founding father of the Impressionist movement. His groundbreaking 1872 painting, Impression, Sunrise, famously gave the movement its name after critics used the term mockingly. Rather than rendering fine details or traditional historical subjects, Monet dedicated his life to capturing the transient effects of light, weather, and color directly from nature (en plein air). In his later decades, he settled in Giverny, where he meticulously designed a water garden that served as the sole model for his iconic, near-abstract Water Lilies (Nymphéas) series

Rembrandt van Rijn

Rembrandt van Rijn (1606–1669) is widely recognized as one of the greatest visual storytellers in history and the defining master of the Dutch Golden Age. Working as a painter, printmaker, and draftsman in Amsterdam, he became legendary for his unrivaled mastery of chiaroscuro—the dramatic use of stark light and deep shadow to evoke profound emotional tension. Rembrandt's expansive body of work ranges from sweeping historical and biblical scenes, like The Night Watch, to roughly 80 deeply honest, self-reflective self-portraits that intimately documented his path from youthful success to late-life financial tragedy.
